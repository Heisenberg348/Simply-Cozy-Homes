How to light a room with no overhead fixture

Three lamps, the right bulb temperature, and a rule about height. That’s the whole trick.

SC Simply Cozy Homes Editorial Team Editorial team · Coming soon · 5 min read

Plenty of rented rooms have one sad wall switch and no ceiling fixture at all. The fix is not a brighter lamp. It is three smaller light sources at different heights, all in the same color of white.

The three-lamp rule

One tall lamp in a corner for general light, one table lamp at seated eye level for reading, and one low light such as a shelf lamp for the evening. Three medium sources beat one bright one because the shadows land in different places.

Bulb temperature matters more than the lamp

Look for 2700K on the box for a warm, cozy light, and keep every bulb in the room the same number. A room with one cool bulb and two warm ones looks broken no matter how nice the lamps are.

Height and placement

Keep the bulb of a reading lamp roughly at shoulder height when you are seated, and never point a bare bulb across the room at eye level. Push lights into corners so they bounce off two walls instead of glaring off one.

One switch for all of it

Three lamps means three plugs, which is annoying every single night. Smart plugs or a single switched extension turn the whole set into one action and are the reason people keep using the lamps.

What not to buy

Skip anything with a cool white fixed bulb, skip ring lights sold as decor, and be careful with floor uplighters in rooms with low ceilings. They flatten a room instead of warming it.
