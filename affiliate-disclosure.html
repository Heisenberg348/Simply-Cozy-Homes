Affiliate Disclosure

Effective date: 3 September 2026

Simply Cozy Homes makes money through affiliate links. You have a right to know that before you take our advice, so here it is in plain terms, without the legal fog most sites bury it in.

The short version

When you click a link on this site and buy something on Amazon, Amazon pays us a small commission. You pay exactly the same price you would have paid without us. Our commission comes out of Amazon’s margin, not your wallet.

What an affiliate link actually does

Our links to Amazon carry a tracking code that identifies this site. When you click one, Amazon stores a small cookie in your browser. If you buy something within Amazon’s tracking window, they credit the sale to us and pay a percentage of it as an advertising fee. That’s the entire mechanism — there’s no markup, no surcharge, and no separate checkout.

Every affiliate link on this site is marked up with the sponsored attribute, which is how we tell search engines the link is commercial. We do that because it’s honest, and because it’s what Google asks for.

Required Amazon statement

Amazon and the Amazon logo are trademarks of Amazon.com, Inc. or its affiliates. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What a commission does not buy

  • It doesn’t buy a place on the site. No brand can pay to be listed, ranked higher, or described more kindly. There is no rate card because there is nothing for sale.
  • It doesn’t change which product we pick. Our rate is effectively the same across the home goods we cover, so recommending a worse product would earn us the same money and cost us your trust.
  • It doesn’t soften a verdict. We name products we think are a waste of money, and we say what we’d skip. Those sentences cost us commissions and stay in anyway.
  • It doesn’t give a brand a preview. No manufacturer reads a page before you do or gets a line removed after publication.

Free samples

We buy most of what we write about. If a company does send us something at no charge, we’ll say so directly on the page where it’s discussed — not in a footnote here. A sample buys a company the possibility of coverage and nothing else. We keep no obligation to publish, and if the item disappoints us we say that instead.

Prices and availability

We deliberately don’t print prices. They change constantly, an out-of-date number is worse than no number, and Amazon’s terms don’t permit displaying a price that isn’t updated automatically. Whatever Amazon shows at the moment you check out is the real price. Availability works the same way — a product we recommend today may be out of stock tomorrow, and that’s outside our control.

What we are not

We are not a store. We hold no inventory, process no payments, ship nothing, and cannot see your order. Your purchase is a contract between you and Amazon, governed by Amazon’s conditions of use, return policy, and warranty terms. Anything to do with delivery, damage, returns, or refunds has to go through Amazon’s customer service, and we genuinely have no ability to help with it.

Other programs

Amazon is currently our only affiliate program. If that changes we’ll list the new program on this page and update the date at the top before any such links go live.

Cookies and your choices

Amazon’s tracking cookie is set by Amazon, not by us, and it’s subject to their privacy notice. You can block or clear cookies in your browser at any time. Doing so means we won’t be credited for a purchase, and nothing about the site or your price changes. Our own data practices are described in our privacy policy.

Why we bother spelling this out

Disclosure is required by the Federal Trade Commission and by Amazon’s own operating agreement, so we’d be doing it regardless. But the practical reason is simpler: our recommendations are only useful if you believe them, and you can’t judge advice without knowing who’s paying for it. If anything on this page seems inconsistent with what you see on the site, tell us.
