Small-kitchen storage that works under 100 sq ft

Most organizing products assume you have space. Here’s what works when you don’t.

SC Simply Cozy Homes Editorial Team Editorial team · September 2026 · 6 min read

Almost every organizing product is photographed in a big kitchen with empty counters. In a small kitchen the rules change: anything that takes up counter space has to earn it twice over, and vertical space stops being optional.

Measure before you buy anything

Take three numbers before you order a single organizer: the depth of your cabinets, the height under your sink, and the free width of your longest counter run. Most returns happen because a product was two centimetres too deep, not because it was badly made.

Vertical space is the space you have

In a small kitchen the walls and the insides of cabinet doors are the only real estate left. Stacking shelf risers, an over-sink rack and a couple of door-mounted holders will usually free more room than any drawer organizer.

Under-sink is the biggest single win

The cupboard under the sink is normally half wasted because of the pipes. A pull-out organizer built around the pipework turns a dark hole you reach into blindly into two usable drawers, and it is the one product we recommend to everyone.

Airtight containers, but only in one size family

Decanting works if the containers stack. Buy one set that nests and matches rather than three different shapes, or you will lose the space you gained to awkward gaps.

What to skip in a small kitchen

Skip lazy Susans in shallow cabinets, oversized utensil crocks, tiered fruit stands, and anything that lives permanently on the counter. In a small kitchen, empty counter is a feature, not wasted space.
