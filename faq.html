Frequently Asked Questions

Everything readers ask us most, answered without the marketing voice.

About the site

Do you sell any of these products yourselves?

No. Simply Cozy Homes is a publication, not a store. We carry no stock, take no payments, and never see your card details. Every product link sends you to Amazon, and your purchase is made there under Amazon’s terms.

The practical consequence is that anything to do with orders, delivery, damage, returns or refunds has to be handled through Amazon’s customer service. We have no access to your order and genuinely can’t look it up for you.

Who writes these guides?

A small editorial team, credited on every article as the Simply Cozy Homes editorial team. We’re not interior designers and we don’t claim to be. We’re people who have furnished small apartments on tight budgets, made a fair number of bad purchases, and got methodical about not repeating them.

Every article carries a byline and a publication date so you always know how current the advice is.

Why do you cover so few categories?

Because covering everything badly is the standard failure of affiliate sites. We stay inside six areas — kitchen tools, storage, decor, lighting, cleaning gear and textiles — and we’d rather go deep on a narrow beat than publish a thin page about garden hoses because someone searches for them.

How we pick things

Do you actually use the products you recommend?

Some of them, and we tell you which. When we’ve lived with something we say how long. When a recommendation is research-based, the page says that too, in those words.

We think that’s more useful than the common alternative, which is implying hands-on testing that never happened. Our full method is on the how we test page.

Why do you read the bad reviews first?

Five-star reviews get written in the first week, when nothing has had time to fail. One- and two-star reviews get written in month eight, and that’s where you learn about the hinge that snaps, the coating that peels, or the drawer unit that doesn’t fit the cabinet it was sold for.

One angry review is a bad unit. Forty describing the same fault at the same point is a design problem, and that rules a product out for us.

Why do your guides only name two or three products?

A list of twenty options isn’t a recommendation, it’s a decision handed back to you. We name the one we’d buy, a cheaper option where we spell out exactly what you give up, and sometimes an upgrade that’s only worth it under a condition we describe. If we can’t pick a winner honestly, we publish the guide and say so.

Do brands pay to appear on the site?

No, and there’s nothing for sale here at any price. We don’t accept sponsored posts, paid placements, link insertions or guest articles. No manufacturer sees a page before you do, and none of them can have a sentence removed after publication.

If we ever accept a free sample, we say so on the page where that product is discussed. A sample buys the possibility of coverage and nothing more.

How often do you revisit older guides?

We check our published picks periodically against recent reviews, because home products get quietly cheapened over time — steel becomes coated aluminum, solid wood becomes veneer, and the listing keeps all its old five-star ratings.

When a pick stops being the pick, we replace it and note the change on the page rather than editing it out silently.

Prices and money

Why don’t you show any prices?

Two reasons. Prices on home goods change weekly, so a number typed into a page is wrong within days and actively misleads you. And Amazon’s operating agreement doesn’t allow displaying a price unless it updates automatically.

So we describe cost in relative terms — the budget pick, the upgrade — and let the live price sit where it’s always accurate, on Amazon’s own listing.

Does clicking your link cost me more?

Not a cent. You pay exactly what you’d pay going to Amazon directly. Our commission is paid by Amazon out of their own margin, which is why the price you see is unaffected.

Doesn’t earning commission bias your picks?

It’s the right question to ask. Our commission rate is effectively the same across the home categories we cover, so pointing you at a worse product would pay us the same and cost us the only thing we have, which is your trust.

You’ll also find products on this site that we tell you to skip, and sections explaining why the popular choice is the wrong one. Those cost us money and stay in anyway. The full arrangement is set out on our affiliate disclosure page.

Buying and using the products

Something I bought arrived damaged. Can you help?

We wish we could, but no. Sign in to Amazon, open Your Orders, and start a return or a replacement there. Amazon is the seller of record and the only party who can resolve it.

Do tell us afterwards, though, if the problem looks like a pattern rather than bad luck in transit. That’s the kind of signal that gets a product pulled from the site.

Will your recommendations work in a rental?

A lot of what we cover is chosen with renters in mind, because damage deposits are a real constraint and most home advice ignores them. Where a product needs drilling, we say so plainly, and we look for a no-holes alternative when one genuinely works.

Check your own lease before mounting anything. Adhesive strips fail on textured or freshly painted walls, and that failure is on you rather than your landlord.

Is a product still worth buying if the guide is a year old?

Check the date at the top of the article, then check the recent reviews on the listing before you buy. If the newest reviews read like they’re describing a different product than the older ones, it probably is a different product, and you should treat the old ratings as worthless.

If you spot that before we do, we’d be grateful if you told us.

Getting in touch

Can I suggest a product or a guide topic?

Please do — it’s our favorite kind of email. Tell us what you own, how long you’ve had it, and what surprised you about it. Long-term ownership detail is exactly what’s missing from most reviews, and a reader tip often beats an hour of research.

Write to info@simplycozyhomes.com.

I think you got something wrong. What now?

Send us the specific line and what you believe is correct. We’ll verify it independently and fix it if you’re right, noting anything significant on the page. We’d much rather be corrected than be quietly wrong.

How long do replies take?

Usually two to three business days. If a week passes with no reply, check your spam folder and then send it again — we’d rather get a duplicate than lose your message.

Still stuck on something? Email the editors and we’ll answer you directly.
